# B2B LinkedIn Organic Growth Playbook

## Quick Summary

This playbook explains how a B2B founder, operator, or consultant can build a LinkedIn presence using organic content only.

It is based on research saved in this repository:

- 10 practitioners
- 49 collected LinkedIn posts
- Source material from `research/`
- Collection window: 17-19 June 2026

Paid distribution is outside the scope of this playbook.

## Important Note

This is a first draft built from the research in this repo and a few independent checks. When a claim was checked outside the repo, it is marked as "verified independently."

The sections on expert disagreements, rejected tactics, and who not to follow are judgment calls based on the evidence. Treat them as a strong starting point, not as final truth.

## Scope And Method

Every recommendation in this playbook connects back to a specific LinkedIn post saved in `research/linkedin-posts/`.

The method was simple:

1. Choose practitioners who actively use LinkedIn.
2. Collect their recent posts.
3. Look for repeated patterns.
4. Compare where experts disagree.
5. Reject tactics that are weak, misleading, or hard to repeat.
6. Turn the strongest findings into a practical SOP.

The focus is LinkedIn organic strategy. This means the playbook does not cover paid ads, YouTube strategy, podcasts, newsletters, or cross-platform content systems.

## The 9-Step SOP

### Step 1: Fix Your Positioning First

**Rule:** Before posting more content, fix your LinkedIn headline, banner, and featured section.

**Why it matters:** If people visit your profile and cannot quickly understand who you help and why you matter, posting more will not solve the problem.

**Source:** Lara Acostar, 12.06.2026 - [link](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/laraacostar_i-built-this-to-share-with-my-private-clients-activity-7470418239987212288-qGa7)

### Step 2: Use Story Before Authority

**Rule:** Start with a real story or specific experience before giving advice.

**Simple structure:**

1. Open with a concrete story.
2. State the lesson.
3. Add proof points or examples.
4. End with a clear question.

**Why it matters:** People trust advice more when they can see where it came from.

**Sources:**

- Matt Barker, 11.06.2026 - [link](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/mattjbarker1_you-can-be-worth-40m-but-still-get-ignored-activity-7468632307122421760-T_tL)
- Lara Acostar, 12.06.2026 - [link](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/laraacostar_theres-100s-of-ways-to-get-more-followers-activity-7469695786713473025-XxGK)

**Important exception:** If you already have strong original data or a strong reputation, you can open with data instead. See [Disagreement 3](#3-story-first-or-data-first).

### Step 3: Build AI Context Before Writing Prompts

**Rule:** If you use AI, create short context files before asking it to help with content.

Useful context files can include:

- Who you are
- Your audience
- Your writing voice
- Your content rules
- What AI should avoid

**Why it matters:** Without context, AI gives generic output. With too much context, the workflow becomes slow and messy. Keep the context useful but lean.

**Sources:**

- Ruben Hassid, 16.06.2026 - [link](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/ruben-hassid_delete-almost-every-prompt-you-ever-saved-activity-7472603969618337793-jn2X)
- Kyle Poyar, 18.06.2026 - [link](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/kyle-poyar_a-4-step-guide-to-build-your-ai-gtm-system-activity-7473001162556964865-qJ8o)

### Step 4: Pick A Default Post Format

**Rule:** Start with short posts under about 100 words, usually with one clear link or action.

**Why it matters:** This is easier to produce consistently. It also works well when your goal is to drive a reader to take one action.

**Source:** Matt Barker ran a 30-day test using short posts, links, and no carousels or infographics. Source: Matt Barker, 16.06.2026 - [link](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/mattjbarker1_i-did-it-i-completed-my-30-day-linkedin-activity-7472618597664813056-4--S)

**When to break the rule:**

- Use infographics when the content is genuinely worth saving, such as a checklist or framework. Source: Eric Partaker, 17.06.2026 - [link](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/ericpartaker_a-full-operations-book-in-one-page-heres-share-7472906868839911425-SH1P)
- Use event or access-based posts when you learned something your audience could not easily learn elsewhere. Source: Ishan Sharma, 14.06.2026 - [link](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/ishansharma7390_business-startup-meta-activity-7471548508517539840-hSIf)

### Step 5: Use Data, But Be Honest About Certainty

**Rule:** Use real data when possible, and clearly say what is confirmed versus what is inferred.

**Why it matters:** Data builds trust, but only if you credit the source and avoid presenting guesses as facts.

Good habits:

- Name the original source of a framework or statistic.
- Do not hide the source in a buried link.
- Label speculation as speculation.
- If you independently checked something, say so.

**Example:** Kyle Poyar credits Christoph Janz's "hunting animals" SaaS framework by name. I independently checked that the framework came from a 2014 Point Nine Capital post and is still widely cited. Sources: Kyle Poyar, 04.06.2026 - [link](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/kyle-poyar_the-initial-decision-about-which-customer-activity-7467928850438627330-guvB), independent check - [medium.com/point-nine-news](https://medium.com/point-nine-news/five-years-later-five-ways-to-build-a-100-million-saas-business-49ebca14825c)

**News caution:** When reacting to breaking industry news, post quickly but carefully. Jess Ramos and Aaron Levie both commented on the Claude Fable 5/Mythos 5 export-control story before Anthropic later confirmed important details. They were mostly right, but the lesson is still this: separate confirmed facts from your own interpretation. Independent check: Anthropic, "Redeploying Claude Fable 5," 30.06.2026 - [anthropic.com/news/redeploying-fable-5](https://www.anthropic.com/news/redeploying-fable-5)

### Step 6: Match The Format To The Idea

**Rule:** Do not force every idea into the same post template.

Use the right shape for the idea:

- A sharp opinion can be 2-5 lines.
- A complex topic may need bullets.
- A personal lesson may need a short story.
- An abstract idea may need a simple real-life example first.

**Sources:**

- Jason Lemkin, 17.06.2026 - [link](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/jasonmlemkin_the-pizza-parlor-picks-up-the-phone-for-a-activity-7473020932102742016-bCFe)
- Aaron Levie, 14.06.2026 - [link](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/boxaaron_the-big-news-last-night-was-that-the-us-government-activity-7471588067246747648-bDv0)
- Saheli Chatterjee, 17.06.2026 - [link](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/saheli-chatterjee-organic-marketing_for-a-month-i-watched-my-sister-obsess-over-activity-7472864729519001601-Zt4o)

### Step 7: Set Rules For AI

**Rule:** Use AI to help extract, organize, and edit ideas. Do not let AI replace your actual point of view.

Best use of AI:

- Ask AI to interview you.
- Let it pull out memories, examples, and patterns.
- Use it to structure notes.
- Write the final point in your own voice.

**Why it matters:** A small account's voice is one of its strongest assets. Generic AI writing makes people sound the same.

**Source:** Matt Barker, 17.06.2026 - [link](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/mattjbarker1_i-built-a-claude-skill-that-finds-the-story-activity-7472980970242109440-YAoe)

### Step 8: Keep The Payoff Close To The Hook

**Rule:** If you promise a resource, make it easy to get.

Good:

- Put the link in the post.
- Put the resource one click away.
- Make the next step obvious.

Weak:

- Subscribe first.
- Wait for an email.
- Search inside the email.
- Open another page.
- Find a folder.
- Download from there.

**Why it matters:** Every extra step creates drop-off. In B2B, readers are busy. Respect their time.

See [Disagreement 1](#1-direct-resource-or-gated-funnel) for the full trade-off.

### Step 9: Review Weekly

**Rule:** Treat your LinkedIn account like a weekly experiment, not just a posting calendar.

Each week, review:

- What did I post?
- What was the hypothesis?
- What result did it get?
- What should I test next?

**Why it matters:** Without review, you are only posting. With review, you are learning.

**Source:** Matt Barker, 16.06.2026 - [link](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/mattjbarker1_i-did-it-i-completed-my-30-day-linkedin-activity-7472618597664813056-4--S)

## Where Experts Disagree

### 1. Direct Resource Or Gated Funnel?

**Ruben Hassid's approach:** Gate the resource. Ask readers to subscribe, wait for an email, find a link, open Notion, and download from there. Source: Ruben Hassid, 15.06.2026 - [link](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/ruben-hassid_how-to-never-be-stuck-on-i-dont-know-how-activity-7472513317341884416-520Q)

**Saheli Chatterjee's approach:** Give the resource directly inside the post. Source: Saheli Chatterjee, 14.06.2026 - [link](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/saheli-chatterjee-organic-marketing_claude-aiformarketers-learnai-activity-7471442923310534656-BN0F)

**My decision:** Default to direct access.

**Why:** A B2B reader's time is valuable. If the resource is simple, do not hide it behind a funnel. But if the resource is a serious toolkit or template library, gating can be fair because the value is higher.

### 2. Short Posts Or Long Infographic Posts?

**Matt Barker's approach:** Use short, link-focused posts. Source: Matt Barker, 16.06.2026 - [link](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/mattjbarker1_i-did-it-i-completed-my-30-day-linkedin-activity-7472618597664813056-4--S)

**Eric Partaker's approach:** Use long, dense posts with strong infographics. Source: Eric Partaker, 17.06.2026 - [link](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/ericpartaker_a-full-operations-book-in-one-page-heres-share-7472906868839911425-SH1P)

**My decision:** Start with short posts as the default.

**Why:** Short posts are easier to produce and test. Infographics can work well, but they need stronger design skill, more time, and a save-worthy idea.

### 3. Story First Or Data First?

**Lara Acostar and Matt Barker's approach:** Open with story. Build trust before teaching. Sources: Lara Acostar, 12.06.2026 - [link](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/laraacostar_theres-100s-of-ways-to-get-more-followers-activity-7469695786713473025-XxGK), Matt Barker, 11.06.2026 - [link](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/mattjbarker1_you-can-be-worth-40m-but-still-get-ignored-activity-7468632307122421760-T_tL)

**Kyle Poyar's approach:** Open with data. His posts often begin with a strong number or dataset. Source: Kyle Poyar, 17.06.2026 - [link](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/kyle-poyar_data-from-1043-companies-70-of-them-still-activity-7472629768253968386-lsZk)

**My decision:** Start with story until you have strong data of your own.

**Why:** Kyle can lead with data because he already has authority. A smaller account needs to earn trust first. Story is the easier credibility tool at the beginning.

### 4. Should AI Write The Post?

**Ruben Hassid's approach:** Use AI heavily for production, including prompts and design workflows. Source: Ruben Hassid, 16.06.2026 - [link](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/ruben-hassid_vibecoding-activity-7472875732130689024-y--k)

**Matt Barker's approach:** Use AI to extract the story, but do not let it write the final post. Source: Matt Barker, 17.06.2026 - [link](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/mattjbarker1_i-built-a-claude-skill-that-finds-the-story-activity-7472980970242109440-YAoe)

**My decision:** Let AI help with research, structure, and editing. Keep the final voice human.

**Why:** AI is useful for production, but the reader should still feel a real person behind the post.

## What I Rejected

### 1. Fake Urgency And Scarcity

**What I saw:** Repeated countdowns, limited-time claims, application gates, password-to-DM mechanics, and dramatic hooks.

**Main source:** Lara Acostar, 17.06.2026 - [link 1](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/laraacostar_im-leaking-my-phone-number-and-im-kinda-activity-7472971360458342400-E4c-), 12.06.2026 - [link 2](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/laraacostar_i-built-this-to-share-with-my-private-clients-activity-7470418239987212288-qGa7)

**Why I rejected it:** B2B buyers usually make slower, more careful decisions. Repeated urgency can reduce trust. There was also no clear evidence in this research that fake scarcity performs better than a simple, always-available link.

### 2. Sponsored Or Brand-Invited Coverage As A Growth Tactic

**What I saw:** Posts that looked like favorable coverage of company launches, events, or products.

**Main source:** Ishan Sharma, 14.06.2026 - [link](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/ishansharma7390_business-startup-meta-activity-7471548508517539840-hSIf), 18.05.2026 - [link](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/ishansharma7390_i-found-the-super-app-for-finance-activity-7458536210269986816-pUoo)

**Why I rejected it:** This is not repeatable for most people starting from zero. Event access usually comes after you already have reach. It is not a reliable early growth strategy.

### 3. Content-Free Curiosity Bait

**What I saw:** A post saying "I'm building something" without giving useful details, paired with an affiliate-style discount link.

**Main source:** Eric Partaker, 16.06.2026 - [link](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/ericpartaker_ad-lovablepartner-activity-7472619034174394369-RAI2)

**Why I rejected it:** The reader is asked to comment or guess, but gets little value. This is weaker than Eric's framework and infographic posts, which are much more useful.

## Original Ideas

### Idea 1: The Hypothesis Ledger

**What it is:** A weekly or biweekly post series where you publish your LinkedIn experiment results.

Each post follows this format:

1. What I tested last time
2. What happened
3. What I learned
4. What I will test next

**Why it could work:**

- It creates original data even for a small account.
- It gives readers a reason to follow the next update.
- It builds trust because you show both wins and losses.
- It avoids fake urgency because the timeline is real.

**Why it is still unproven:** I have not tested it myself. It is an original idea based on patterns in the research, especially Matt Barker's 30-day experiment and Kyle Poyar's data-led style.

### Idea 2: Source-Skepticism As Content

**What it is:** When you cite a stat or claim, add one line showing what you checked.

Example:

> I checked this against the original company report. I could confirm the total sample size, but not the exact segment breakdown.

**Why it could work:**

- It builds trust.
- It makes your thinking visible.
- It separates you from people who repeat numbers without checking them.
- It fits naturally with the Hypothesis Ledger.

## Limits Of This Playbook

This playbook is useful, but it has limits.

- **Small sample:** It uses 10 accounts and 49 posts.
- **Short collection window:** The posts were collected around 17-19 June 2026.
- **Self-reported numbers:** Many follower, revenue, and impression claims come from the creators themselves.
- **Selling bias:** Many experts sell courses, communities, consulting, or newsletters.
- **LinkedIn-only scope:** This does not cover paid ads or other platforms.
- **Not personally tested:** The recommendations are based on observed public behavior, not my own live account results.
- **Practitioner-heavy sample:** The research focuses on creator-operators, not in-house B2B marketing teams.

Use this playbook as a starting system. Improve it with your own results.

## Who I Would Not Recommend Following

### Lara Acostar

**Recommendation:** Do not use her as a primary model for a B2B founder.

**Why:** Her collected posts rely heavily on urgency mechanics: countdowns, application gates, password-to-DM systems, and dramatic hooks. Her storytelling framework is useful, but similar storytelling patterns appear in Saheli Chatterjee's and Matt Barker's posts without the same scarcity layer.

**Better alternative for storytelling:** Saheli Chatterjee or Matt Barker.

### Ishan Sharma

**Recommendation:** Follow with caution.

**Why:** The collected posts lean heavily toward event or brand-invited coverage. That may be useful once someone already has reach, but it is weaker as a model for someone trying to build organic LinkedIn growth from zero.

**Better alternative for cleaner B2B/AI/content signals:** Kyle Poyar or Matt Barker.
